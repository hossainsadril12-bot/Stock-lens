## How to use this document

This is not a summary of the PRD. The PRD already lists *what* to build.

This document answers three things the PRD does not:

1. **Who** actually touches each feature, and how often
2. **Where** the feature list runs out before the human job is finished
3. **What** we will do about it

Read in this order: personas → feature list → journeys → problems → principles → **ideate** → **user flows**.
Everything after the journeys was *derived* from them, not invented. The Ideate section (13) was derived from the eight defined problems. The User Flows section (14) was derived from the four Ideate concepts.

**One-line design brief for this project:**

> The system knows more than it says. Our job is to make it speak.

## 1. The people

These are working personas built from the PRD's three roles. They are **assumptions** until validated with real users.

### Rahim, 26 — StockLens Staff

Warehouse assistant at a printing business.

| | |
|---|---|
| Device | Cracked Android phone. No laptop. |
| Posture | Standing, walking, one hand holding a box |
| Environment | Noisy loading bay, poor light, weak signal |
| Tech comfort | Uses WhatsApp fluently. Has never used inventory software. |
| Types | Slowly, with typos |
| Attention | Seconds. Someone is usually waiting for him. |
| Uses daily | Search, scan, stock in/out, check his location's stock |
| Cannot | Delete items, change prices, approve anything |

**What he needs:** speed, big targets, minimum typing, an answer without navigating.

### Mr. Anwar, 48 — StockLens Admin

Owner of the business.

| | |
|---|---|
| Device | Laptop for setup. Phone for everything else. |
| Two modes | **Setup** (once, sitting, patient) and **Daily** (30 seconds, standing, impatient) |
| Tech comfort | Excellent with Excel. Suspicious of software. |
| Attention | 15 minutes on day one. 40 seconds every morning after. |
| Uses | Everything |
| Special power | The only person who can approve POs above threshold |

**What he needs:** a fast setup that proves value early, then a glanceable daily check he can act on from his phone.

> **Critical insight:** Setup-Anwar and Daily-Anwar need opposite designs. Same person, same role, two products.

### Mr. Salam, 55 — StockLens Viewer

Investor in one of the client's buildings.

| | |
|---|---|
| Frequency | Twice a month |
| Memory of the app | Zero, every time |
| Wants | One number, then to leave |
| Access | Read-only |

**What he needs:** screens that explain themselves. No learned behaviour, no hidden menus.

## 5. User journeys

Five journeys, chosen by **frequency × pain**. Each one uses the same structure:

> Trigger → Context → Steps (does · thinks · feels) → Moments of truth → What it demands

Emotion key: 🙂 fine · 😐 neutral · 😕 confused · 😟 worried · 😠 frustrated · 😰 alarmed · 😩 defeated

---

### Journey 1 — Anwar's first twenty minutes

**Trigger:** He has just paid for VantaTrack and wants to see if it was worth it.

**Context:** Monday 9 pm, at home, laptop. 400 items in his head and an old Excel file. He will give this about **fifteen minutes** before deciding it is too complicated.

| # | What he does | What he thinks | Feel |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Opens StockLens for the first time | *"Okay… where do I start?"* | 😐 |
| 2 | Sees 14 empty menu items | *"Groups? Cycle counts? What is all this?"* | 😟 |
| 3 | Clicks Items — blank page | *"Is it broken, or just empty?"* | 😟 |
| 4 | Clicks Add Item | *"It wants an item type. Which one is a flat?"* | 😕 |
| 5 | Fills the form, saves | *"Done. Three minutes."* | 🙂 |
| 6 | Sees the item — **0 in stock** | *"It didn't save the stock. Did I do it wrong?"* | 😠 |
| 7 | Thinks about the other 399 | *"Three minutes each. That's twenty hours."* | 😰 |
| 8 | Considers closing the tab | *"Maybe I'll do this later."* | 😔 |

**Moments of truth**

- **Step 2** — fourteen unexplained menu items is the first impression of the entire product
- **Step 6** — the predicted "0 stock" confusion, live. Trust breaks here.
- **Step 7** — the *multiplication* defeats him, not the form

**What it demands** — S1 onboarding · hide irrelevant features by business type · S4 success screen · offer Import the moment bulk work is implied

> The whole journey turns on one thing: **he must reach a small win before minute five.**

---

### Journey 2 — Anwar adds one item on a normal Tuesday

**Trigger:** A new flat came onto the books.

**Context:** Six months in, 400 items, knows the app. Laptop. 30 seconds of attention.

| # | What he does | What he thinks | Feel |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Items → Add Item | *"Quick one."* | 🙂 |
| 2 | Picks type: real estate | *"Good, the form changed to flat fields."* | 🙂 |
| 3 | Fills name, group, area, price | *"Basically the same as the last one."* | 😐 |
| 4 | Uploads two photos | *"Wait — was it 1150 or 1200 sq ft?"* | 😕 |
| 5 | Saves | | 🙂 |
| 6 | Wants to add the flat next door | *"Do I type all twelve fields again?"* | 😠 |

**Moments of truth**

- **Step 2** — the form must visibly change per type. Showing "batch tracking" on a flat destroys confidence in the whole product.
- **Step 4** — he leaves the form to check a fact. **Does he lose his work?**
- **Step 6** — the repeat problem in miniature. Two flats, not sixty. Still annoying.

**What it demands** — type first then a tailored form · draft autosave or a leave-warning · S5 "Add another like this"

### Journey 3 — Rahim finds an item

**Trigger:** A customer at the counter asks: *"Do you have A4 premium paper? I need 180 sheets."*

**Context:** Standing, customer watching, phone in one hand, noisy room. He has roughly **15 seconds** before it gets awkward.

| # | What he does | What he thinks | Feel |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Opens StockLens on his phone | *"Come on, load."* | 😐 |
| 2 | Taps search | *"Where is it — top? menu?"* | 😕 |
| 3 | Types "papr" (hurrying) | *"No results?! It's definitely there."* | 😠 |
| 4 | Retypes "paper" | | 😐 |
| 5 | Sees 6 paper items | *"Which one is premium?"* | 😕 |
| 6 | Opens the right one | *"200. Good."* | 🙂 |
| 7 | Tells the customer yes | | 🙂 |
| 8 | **Later:** 50 were reserved | *"I promised something I don't have."* | 😰 |

**Moments of truth**

- **Step 3** — one typo makes the product look empty. A typo is not rare when someone is hurrying.
- **Step 5** — six similar results with nothing to distinguish them
- **Step 8** — the disaster. **The system was correct; the screen misled him.**

**What it demands** — S2 typo-tolerant multi-field search · one-tap access · the number in the result row · **S3 available-first**

> Step 8 is the most expensive moment in the entire product, and it is decided by a single design choice: which number to display.

### Journey 4 — Rahim receives a delivery

**Trigger:** A truck arrives with 20 cartons. The driver wants to leave.

**Context:** Loading bay, weak signal, one hand free, boxes stacked around him, a real human standing there creating time pressure.

| # | What he does | What he thinks | Feel |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Finds the right PO | *"Three orders this week — which is this?"* | 😟 |
| 2 | Counts the cartons | *"Eighteen. Not twenty."* | 😕 |
| 3 | Looks for a way to record 18 | *"Can I even receive part of it?"* | 😰 |
| 4 | Scans carton 1 | *"Good, that worked."* | 🙂 |
| 5 | Gets bounced to a detail page | *"Now back… and scan again."* | 😐 |
| 6 | Repeats 17 more times | *"This is painful."* | 😠 |
| 7 | Types batch number + expiry | *"I can't read this print."* | 😩 |
| 8 | Signal drops mid-way | *"Did any of that save?"* | 😰 |
| 9 | Confirms | *"Is finance told, or do I call them?"* | 😐 |

**Moments of truth**

- **Step 3** — partial delivery is **normal life**, not an edge case
- **Step 6** — the back-button loop. Multiply any small friction by 20.
- **Step 8** — the PRD says nothing about what happens here
- **Step 9** — the PO → Spend sync is invisible to him. Should it be?

**What it demands** — partial receipt as the default form · S9 continuous scan · scan the expiry date · offline queue with a visible count · X6 quiet sync confirmation

### Journey 5 — Anwar's morning check

**Trigger:** 8:15 am, in the car, before the office.

**Context:** Phone. **Forty seconds.** He wants to know one thing: *is anything wrong today?*

| # | What he does | What he thinks | Feel |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Opens the dashboard | *"What needs me today?"* | 😐 |
| 2 | Sees 10 widgets of equal size | *"Which of these matters?"* | 😕 |
| 3 | Scrolls hunting for red | *"Where are the problems?"* | 😕 |
| 4 | Finds a low stock alert | *"How long has that been there?"* | 😟 |
| 5 | Wants to act on it | *"Do I have to go to another screen?"* | 😐 |
| 6 | Sees 2 POs awaiting approval | *"Can I approve from here?"* | 😕 |
| 7 | Closes the app | *"I'll deal with it at the office."* | 😐 |

**Moments of truth**

- **Step 2** — ten equal widgets means nothing has priority, so nothing gets attention
- **Step 4** — no sense of *when* or *how urgent*
- **Step 7** — he postponed. **The dashboard failed at its only job.**

**What it demands** — urgent zone on top, large · approve without leaving the dashboard · time context on alerts ("low for 3 days") · a Phase 1 dashboard that shows setup progress instead of empty widgets

## 6. What the journeys revealed

### Finding 1 — Every journey has one 😰 and they are all the same shape

| Journey | The 😰 moment | Cause |
|---|---|---|
| Day one | *"twenty hours of typing"* | No path shown |
| Add item | *"do I retype everything?"* | No repeat shortcut |
| Find item | *"I promised stock I don't have"* | Wrong number shown |
| Delivery | *"did it save?"* | No feedback |
| Morning | *"I'll do it later"* | No priority |

**None of these is a missing feature.** Every one is a communication failure.

### Finding 2 — Nobody wants to use this product

Anwar wants his fifteen minutes back. Rahim wants the customer served. Every journey is someone trying to **get out** of the app. Speed and clarity beat richness.

### Finding 3 — Three journeys break at the same place: the number

Available vs on hand vs reserved appears in Journeys 1, 3 and 4. **S3 is the single highest-value decision in this project.**

### Finding 4 — Multiplication is the real enemy

400 items × 3 minutes. 20 cartons × 1 back-button. Small friction is invisible in a demo and fatal in real use.

> Design rule that follows: for every screen, ask *"how many times a day?"* — then multiply the friction.

## 7. The eight real problems

Thirteen painful moments compressed into eight problem statements.

### Problem 1 — The screen shows a number the user cannot act on 🔴

- **Who:** Rahim, in front of a customer
- **Pain:** Sees 200, promises 200, 50 were reserved
- **Root cause:** Three numbers exist; the design never chose one
- **Solution:** **Available is primary everywhere.** On hand secondary and grey. Reserved on detail only.
- **Success measure:** A user can answer *"can I sell this?"* without opening anything
- **Cost of ignoring:** A real broken promise to a real customer. The most expensive problem in the product.

### Problem 2 — Day one has no path 🔴

- **Who:** Every new company, first 15 minutes
- **Pain:** 14 empty menus, no starting point, twenty hours of implied typing
- **Root cause:** The PRD describes a running system, never an empty one
- **Solution:** S1 — three questions, then one next step. Hide everything the answers made irrelevant.
- **Success measure:** Something real on screen within 5 minutes
- **Cost of ignoring:** The customer leaves before the product is ever tested

### Problem 3 — Doing the same thing twice is not faster 🔴

- **Who:** Anwar, entering item 2 … 400
- **Pain:** Every item costs the same as the first
- **Root cause:** The form has no memory
- **Solution:** S5 "Add another like this" · offer Import wherever bulk work is detected
- **Success measure:** The second item takes under 30 seconds
- **Cost of ignoring:** Abandonment during setup, before any value is seen

### Problem 4 — Tasks end without saying what happened 🔴

- **Who:** Everyone
- **Pain:** *"It shows 0 — is it broken?"* · *"Signal dropped — did it save?"* · *"Does finance know?"*
- **Root cause:** The system does invisible work and says nothing
- **Solution:** S4 — every task ends in a confirmation with a next step. Offline shows *"3 items waiting to sync."*
- **Success measure:** Nobody repeats an action out of uncertainty

### Problem 5 — Small friction multiplied becomes unbearable 🟡

- **Who:** Rahim, scanning 20 cartons
- **Pain:** One extra tap × 20 = a task he starts avoiding
- **Root cause:** The design was imagined once; the task happens twenty times
- **Solution:** S9 continuous scan · S10 count mode
- **Success measure:** 20 cartons = 20 scans and zero navigations

### Problem 6 — Everything looks equally important 🟡

- **Who:** Anwar, 40 seconds in the car
- **Pain:** Ten equal widgets → cannot find the problem → postpones → nothing gets done
- **Root cause:** A dashboard built from *available data* rather than *the user's question*
- **Solution:** Two zones — "Needs you today" (large, actionable) above statistics (small, grey). Add time context.
- **Success measure:** He can act on the most urgent thing in under 15 seconds

### Problem 7 — Search fails exactly when it is needed most 🟡

- **Who:** Rahim, hurrying, customer waiting
- **Pain:** "papr" returns nothing; the product looks empty and broken
- **Root cause:** Search was never specified, so it became a plain text match
- **Solution:** S2 — multi-field, typo-tolerant, one tap, answer in the row
- **Success measure:** A hurried person with a typo still finds the item

### Problem 8 — Real life is not the happy path 🔴

- **Who:** Everyone, constantly
- **Pain:** 18 arrived instead of 20 · signal dropped · two agents clicked the same flat
- **Root cause:** The PRD describes what happens when things go right
- **Solution:** Partial receipt as the **default** form · S12 conflict recovery with alternatives · offline queue with a visible count
- **Success measure:** When something goes wrong, the user still knows what to do next

## 8. The root problem

All eight problems share one cause:

| Problem | What actually failed |
|---|---|
| 1. Wrong number | System knew 150. Screen said 200. |
| 2. No day one | System knew he had nothing. Said nothing. |
| 3. No repeat speed | System knew the last values. Did not offer them. |
| 4. Silent tasks | System did the work. Did not mention it. |
| 5. Friction × 20 | System knew he was scanning again. Made him navigate. |
| 6. Flat dashboard | System knew what was urgent. Showed it same-size. |
| 7. Search fails | System had the item. Would not admit it. |
| 8. Unhappy path | System handled it. Screen did not explain it. |

> ### The system knows more than it says.

The database has everything. The engineering is sound. Every failure is the screen refusing to speak.

**Therefore the design brief is not "add features". It is "make the system speak."**

## 9. Design principles

Five rules, each earned from a real journey moment. Test every screen against all five.

| # | Principle | Born from |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Show the number they can act on** | Problem 1 |
| 2 | **Never end a task on a dead end** | Problems 2, 4 |
| 3 | **The second time must be faster** | Problems 3, 5 |
| 4 | **Urgent beats complete on any glance screen** | Problem 6 |
| 5 | **Say what happened invisibly** | Problems 4, 8 |

**Bonus test, applied to every field on every form:**

> Is the user typing something the system already knows? If yes, it is a design failure.

## 13. Ideate — solution generation (COMPLETE)

**Stage status:** ✅ Complete
**Input:** the eight defined problems (Section 7) and the five design principles (Section 9).
**Output:** one chosen solution per problem, a priority ranking, and four reusable design concepts that carry into the user-flow stage.

**What Ideate is, and what it is not.** Ideate is where we generate and then choose solution ideas. It is *not* the user-flow stage and *not* wireframing. The method here has four moves: reframe each problem as a "How Might We" question, diverge by listing several possible ideas, converge by choosing one and stating why, then state what the chosen idea becomes on a screen. Everything below traces back to a problem in Section 7 — nothing was invented.

---

### 13.1 The eight problems reframed as "How Might We" questions

Reframing turns a complaint into an invitation to design.

1. How might we show one number that instantly answers *"can I sell this?"*
2. How might we give a brand-new user a first win in under five minutes?
3. How might we make the second, third, and fourth entry far faster than the first?
4. How might we make every action end with proof that it worked and a clear next step?
5. How might we let a repeated physical task (scanning, counting) happen with zero navigation between repeats?
6. How might we let a busy owner spot the single most urgent thing in one glance?
7. How might we make search succeed even for a hurried person who mistypes?
8. How might we treat the messy real world (partial delivery, lost signal, two people one unit) as the normal case, not the exception?

---

### 13.2 Diverge and converge, one problem at a time

#### Problem 1 — the number the user can act on

**Ideas considered**
- Show all three numbers (on hand, reserved, available) side by side and let the user decide.
- Show only "available" as a large number and hide the rest.
- Show "available" large, with on hand as small grey text beneath, and reserved only inside the detail view.
- Use a colour dot (green / amber / red) instead of a number.

**Chosen idea** — Available large; on hand small and grey beneath it; reserved only on the detail screen.
**Why** — Showing all three re-creates the exact confusion that caused the broken promise in Journey 3. A colour dot loses the actual quantity Rahim needs to answer "180 sheets?". The chosen idea speaks the one truth that matters while keeping the full breakdown one tap away. It also matches the already-settled Decision 3.
**Becomes on screen** — Every item row and every item header leads with the available number. The detail screen carries the full on hand / reserved / available breakdown.

#### Problem 2 — day one has a path

**Ideas considered**
- A long setup wizard covering every setting.
- A three-question gate: what do you track → where do you keep it → add your first item or import.
- Drop the user straight into an empty Items list with one big "add your first item" button.
- A checklist dashboard that fills in as they complete setup.

**Chosen idea** — The three-question gate, followed immediately by either adding one item or importing many.
**Why** — A long wizard fights Anwar's fifteen-minute patience limit. An empty list gives no sense of what the product is for. The three questions do real work: they let the product hide every feature that does not apply to this business, which directly kills the "fourteen unexplained menus" shock from Journey 1.
**Becomes on screen** — A short onboarding screen with three questions, then a fork to Add Item or Import. The answers reshape the sidebar so a flat-selling company never sees "batch tracking".

#### Problem 3 — the second time is faster

**Ideas considered**
- A duplicate button that clones the last item fully.
- An "add another like this" button that reopens the form pre-filled, ready to edit the few fields that differ.
- Detect repeated work and suggest CSV import instead.
- Templates the user builds once and reuses.

**Chosen idea** — "Add another like this" pre-filled form, plus a prompt to import when the system notices many similar entries.
**Why** — A full clone risks creating unchecked duplicates. Templates are extra setup work Anwar will not do on day one. The pre-filled form keeps him in control while removing the retyping that made Journey 2 painful, and the import nudge handles the truly bulk case.
**Becomes on screen** — The success screen after saving offers "add another like this". When several similar items are added in a row, a quiet banner offers Import.

#### Problem 4 — every task says what happened

**Ideas considered**
- A small toast message that appears and disappears.
- A dedicated success screen that confirms what was saved and offers the next step.
- A silent update with just a checkmark.
- A status line for background work such as "expense sent to finance".

**Chosen idea** — A success screen with an explicit next step for meaningful actions, plus quiet one-line confirmations for invisible background work.
**Why** — A disappearing toast is missed by a hurried user and offers no next step, which is why Journey 1 ended in doubt. The success screen closes the loop and points forward, satisfying "never end on a dead end". The quiet line handles the invisible sync from Journey 4 so finance-sync doubt disappears.
**Becomes on screen** — After add, import, or receive, a confirmation state that names the result and shows the next action. Background syncs surface a single calm line of text.

#### Problem 5 — repeated physical tasks with no navigation

**Ideas considered**
- Keep the current scan-then-detail-page loop but add a fast back button.
- A continuous scan mode where the camera stays open and each scan adds to a running list.
- A count mode that shows one item per screen with a big number pad.
- Voice entry.

**Chosen idea** — Continuous scan mode for receiving, and a one-item-per-screen count mode for counting.
**Why** — A faster back button still multiplies twenty times, which Finding 4 says is fatal. Voice fails in Rahim's noisy loading bay. Keeping the camera open removes the navigation entirely, so twenty cartons become twenty scans and nothing else.
**Becomes on screen** — A scan screen where results stack into a list without leaving the camera. (Count mode is Phase 4, so it is logged but not flowed now.)

#### Problem 6 — the glance dashboard

**Ideas considered**
- Keep ten equal widgets but colour the urgent ones.
- Split the dashboard into two zones: "needs you today" large on top, statistics small below.
- Show only alerts and hide statistics entirely.
- A single-sentence summary: "two things need you today".

**Chosen idea** — Two zones: an urgent action zone on top, statistics demoted below, and time context on every alert. In Phase 1 the top zone shows setup progress instead of empty widgets.
**Why** — Colouring ten equal widgets still forces a hunt. Hiding statistics entirely removes value Anwar sometimes wants. The two-zone split gives priority a physical location on the screen, which is what "urgent beats complete" demands, and it lets a brand-new, empty account show progress rather than ten blank boxes.
**Becomes on screen** — A dashboard with a clear top band of things needing action (each with "low for three days"-style time context and an inline action) and a quieter statistics band beneath.

#### Problem 7 — search that forgives

**Ideas considered**
- Exact text match only.
- Multi-field search across name, SKU, barcode, serial, and batch in one box.
- Typo tolerance so "papr" still finds "paper".
- Recent items shown when the box is empty, and the answer (available number) shown inside each result row.

**Chosen idea** — All of the good parts combined: one box searching every field, typo-tolerant, recent items when empty, and the available number visible in the result row.
**Why** — Journey 3 broke in three separate places (the typo, the six lookalike results, the wrong number). One strong search idea fixes all three at once — high value for low added complexity.
**Becomes on screen** — A prominent one-tap search on the Items list. Results carry enough detail (available number, a distinguishing attribute) to choose without opening anything.

#### Problem 8 — the unhappy path is the default

**Ideas considered**
- Keep the happy-path form and bolt on error messages when things go wrong.
- Make partial receipt the default form that asks "how many arrived?" instead of "confirm 20".
- Design a conflict recovery message that names who took the unit and offers alternatives.
- An offline queue that shows how many items are waiting to sync.

**Chosen idea (Phase 1)** — Make "how many arrived?" the default receiving question, and treat error and partial states as first-class, not afterthoughts.
**Why** — Bolting error messages onto a happy path is what the PRD implicitly does, and it fails Rahim in Journey 4. Designing the partial case as the default matches how deliveries actually behave. The conflict message and offline queue are real and important, but they belong to later phases (conflict needs Pulse in Phase 3; offline is not in the PRD yet), so they are logged, not flowed now.
**Becomes on screen** — Receiving is out of Phase 1 scope, so for now this mainly informs the Import flow: the editable preview that fixes the twelve bad rows on screen instead of forcing a full re-upload is the Phase 1 expression of "unhappy path as default".

### 14.2 Anwar — Admin, Setup mode. Day one. (He builds the world.)

**Who he is:** 48, owns Anwar Supplies & Properties. Excellent with Excel, deeply suspicious of software. On day one he sits at a laptop, patient, with about **fifteen minutes** of goodwill before he decides the thing is too complicated and closes the tab. In his head: a warehouse of paper stock *and* 24 flats in Rio Tower.

**What he needs:** a setup that proves value *fast* — a real win before minute five — and that doesn't drown him by showing warehouse jargon on a flat, or flat jargon on a carton.

**14.2.1 — The first screen is a question, then a switch he controls.** Anwar logs in for the first time. In the old world he'd have hit fourteen empty menu items — *Groups? Cycle counts? Batch tracking?* — and started drowning. Instead onboarding opens with **three plain questions**, one at a time: (1) *What do you track?* — he ticks **two**, **physical products** *and* **real estate**, so he never has to pretend to be only one kind of business; (2) *Where do you keep it?* — he adds his **warehouse** and **Rio Tower** as two locations; (3) *Add your first one now, or bring your list in?* `[A1 Onboarding + S1 · Problem 2 · Guided Empty State]` Because he picked two, the dashboard gains a **mode switch** — a clear **Warehouse ⇄ Real Estate** toggle at the top — and it stays there for good: any time he flips it, the table, columns and required fields reshape to that industry, and he can flip back whenever he likes. Paper mode speaks quantities, batches, reorder points; Real Estate mode speaks statuses (available / reserved / sold) and never a "batch number." The fourteen-menu shock is gone, and nothing is locked at setup — the switch is his to change forever. `[A1 + Q4 mode switch · Problem 2 · Guided Empty State]`

> This is the mode switch doing its hardest job: **two businesses in one company, and Anwar decides which one he's looking at, whenever he wants.** `[Decision 12]`

**14.2.2 — His first item, and the moment trust usually breaks.** He starts in **Warehouse mode**. The Add form is **already shaped for a physical product** — its required fields are Name + Location + Quantity, plus SKU, unit of measure, reorder point — not a generic form with flat fields bolted on. `[A2 + Q5 smart fields · Problem 2 · Guided Empty State]` *(Decision C + D.)* He saves. In the old world this is where trust snapped: the item read **"0 in stock"** and he thought *did I do it wrong?* Here, saving lands on a **success screen that names what happened** and offers next steps as buttons — **"Add stock now,"** **"Add another like this,"** **"Import the rest from a file."** `[A2 + S4 · Problem 4 · Closing Loop]` The "0 stock" panic is defused: the screen explains the state, and **"Add stock now"** — a scan or quantity entry — gives the zero somewhere to go. `[Q1 scan stock-in]` *(Decision A.)*

**14.2.3 — The second item, killing the twenty-hour fear.** Old fear: *retype every field again?* The multiplication (400 × 3 min = twenty hours) is what defeated him, not any single form. He taps **"Add another like this"**; the form reopens **pre-filled**, he edits only the few fields that differ, and the second item takes under thirty seconds. `[A2 + S5 · Problem 3 · Closing Loop]` After a couple in a row, a **quiet banner** offers import — exactly when the bulk case reveals itself. `[S5 → Import nudge · Problem 3 · Closing Loop]`

**14.2.4 — Bringing in the whole warehouse.** He uploads his 400-row spreadsheet and lands on an **editable preview** — his own rows, twelve marked red. Old world: one bad row bounced the whole file. Here he **fixes the twelve red rows on screen** and imports, with a progress bar and a clear duplicate rule. `[A3 + S6 · Problem 8 · Honest Path]` It ends with a named result: *"388 imported, 12 fixed, 0 skipped."* `[A3 + S4 · Problem 4 · Closing Loop]`

**14.2.5 — Then he sets up Rio Tower — one flip of the switch.** He taps the mode switch to **Real Estate**, and the whole module reshapes: the table, columns and Add form all change with it. The form here is *not* the paper form — no SKU, no reorder point. Its required fields are Name + Building/Unit + **Status** (available / reserved / sold), plus floor, area, price. Real estate speaks **status, not quantity.** `[Q4 mode switch + Q5 smart fields · Problem 2 · Guided Empty State]` He doesn't create 24 units one at a time: a **bulk generator** takes the building, the floor range and units-per-floor and creates them all at once, ready to edit. `[Q9 bulk unit generator]` *(Decision resolved — this was the old abandonment risk.)*

> **Resolved (Q9):** 24 — or 60 — units one at a time was the abandonment risk. The owner chose a **bulk generator** over CSV-only, so the risk is closed, not just flagged.

**What Anwar's setup proves:** the **admin mode switch** let one company be two businesses without either side lying, and Anwar controls it — he flips to whichever business he's working on; he never saw a warehouse field on a flat; the **second item was faster than the first**; and the world Rahim and Salam now live in was built by these choices.

### 14.3 Rahim — Staff. A full shift inside Anwar's warehouse.

**Who he is:** 26, Anwar's warehouse assistant on the **paper side**. Stands, walks, one hand always holding a box. Noisy bay, poor light, weak signal, types with typos, someone always waiting. He can search, scan, move stock, and — because staff-can-create-PO is **ON by default** — **raise a reorder** (every one still needs Anwar's sign-off); he cannot delete, price, or approve. `[Roles per PRD §4 + Q11/Q12]`

> **Platform note (Q7):** at launch Rahim works this shift at a **desktop terminal** in the bay. The one-handed cracked-Android version described below is the **later-phase mobile target** — same flow, phone-first, when mobile ships.

**14.3.1 — Morning: he finds one item for a waiting customer.** A customer asks: *"A4 premium paper, 180 sheets?"* Fifteen seconds before it's awkward. He opens StockLens; it opens straight onto stock **at his own location** — with an **All-locations** option one tap away. `[R4 · Problem 2 · Guided Empty State]` *(Decision B.)* The search box is fat, top, already focused; he thumbs **"papr"** — wrong — but the list narrows to paper instead of going blank, and **category filters** (Paper, Ink, Packaging…) sit alongside to cut the noise. `[R1 typo-tolerant + Q10 category/filter · Problem 7 · Honest Path]` Six results; each row carries a distinguishing line **and** a big number: **"150 available."** `[R4 · Problem 1 · Speaking Number]` He reads A4 Premium — **150 available** — opens nothing, and tells the customer *"150 today, not 180."* `[Problem 1 success measure · Speaking Number]` The old disaster (promise 200, discover 50 reserved) never happens, because the row already showed the honest number.
>
> **If it isn't in his location — he Explores, doesn't guess.** When a customer wants an item his warehouse is out of, Rahim taps **Explore** and sees **other locations' stock, read-only**, labelled by location. `[Q2 cross-location Explore + Q3 read-only]` If Chittagong has it, he doesn't promise it himself — he raises a **transfer request to Anwar that names the source warehouse** to pull from. He never taps a button that sells stock he can't ship. `[Q3 transfer request · Problem 7 · Honest Path]`

> The single most expensive moment in the product (Finding 3). It turns entirely on *which number is on the row.* We chose available.

**14.3.2 — Mid-morning: a delivery truck arrives.** Cartons against one of Anwar's POs; driver in a hurry; one hand free, one bar of signal. *(Decision A — scan-based stock-in — applies to this whole scene.)* The product helps him pick the right delivery — open deliveries, supplier and date, newest first. `[R6 · Problem 8 · Honest Path]` He counts **eighteen, not twenty.** The form asks **"How many arrived?"** with the ordered number as a hint, not "confirm 20." He types 18. `[R6 · Problem 8 · Honest Path]` He taps scan **once**; the camera stays open; carton after carton stacks onto a running list — never bounced away and back. `[R2 + S9 · Problem 5 · Honest Path]` A torn barcode offers **manual entry** and *"Not found — create it?"* `[R2 failure path · Problem 8 · Honest Path]` Tiny print, so he **scans the expiry**. `[R5 · Problem 8 · Honest Path]` Signal dies; the screen stays honest: **"6 items waiting to sync,"** counting down on its own when signal returns. `[Problem 4 + 8 · Closing Loop]` He taps **Confirm**: *"18 of 20 received,"* plus a quiet *"Expense sent to Spend."* `[X6 · Problem 4 · Closing Loop]`

> **Open gap (Decision 6, still open):** when 18 ≠ 20, does the PO stay open, close short, or flag? Benchmark against B2B receiving tools (Zoho, Cin7, inFlow). First thing the diagram pass must resolve.

**14.3.3 — Afternoon: a quick stock-out.** A colleague pulls 20 sheets. Rahim finds it the same fast way, taps **stock out**; because he reached it by scanning, the big number pad is already there — no retyping. `[R3 · Problem 5 · Closing Loop]` Available updates instantly; the ledger write is invisible but the screen confirms it happened. `[X5 · Problem 4 · Closing Loop]`

**14.3.4 — Late afternoon: paper runs low, and Rahim hands work up to Anwar.** The A4 Premium is nearly gone. StockLens flags it — Rahim gets the same **low-stock notification** the owner does. `[X1 · Problem 6]` *(PRD 6.4: Staff receive it too.)* The low-stock row has a one-tap **"Create PO,"** pre-filled with supplier, item and a suggested quantity — no blank form. `[Low-stock one-click Create PO · Problem 3 · Closing Loop]` Staff-can-create-PO is **ON by default**, so Rahim raises it without waiting on anyone to enable him. `[Q11 · Reorder / A14]` Then the one rule, with no branch to remember:

- **Every staff-raised PO goes to Pending Approval and up to Anwar — always, whatever the amount.** No auto-send to the supplier, no threshold to reason about. Rahim gets a clear "sent for approval" confirmation, not silence. `[Q12 · PRD 6.6 approval · Problem 4 · Closing Loop]`

> **Resolved (Q12):** the old PRD path let a below-threshold staff PO auto-send with no human sign-off. The owner closed that — **all** staff POs need Admin approval. Simpler to explain, safer with money.

Rahim's shift ends here. **The PO he raised is now sitting in Anwar's world** — where the next story begins. This is the hand-off that makes the two people one scene.

### 14.4 Anwar — Admin, Daily mode. Next morning. (He receives Rahim's work.)

**Same person, opposite product.** 8:15 a.m., on his **phone**, in the car, forty seconds of attention. He wants one thing: *is anything wrong today, on either side of my business?*

**14.4.1 — One glance, one answer, across both halves.** He opens the dashboard. Old world: ten equal widgets, so he scrolled and told himself *"I'll deal with it at the office."* Now the screen has **two zones, not equal**: a large, dark **"Needs you today"** band on top, statistics small and grey below. `[A11 · Problem 6 · Guided Empty State]` This morning the top band shows, with **time context**: *"A4 paper — low for 3 days"* (warehouse) and **"1 purchase order waiting for your approval"** — **the exact PO Rahim raised yesterday.** `[X1 + PRD 6.6 · Problem 6 · Closing Loop]` One dashboard, both halves of his company.

**14.4.2 — Acting without leaving: he approves Rahim's PO.** Each urgent row carries its **action inline.** Anwar taps **Approve** right there, confirms, and it clears — on the warehouse floor that order is now *Sent* to the supplier. The loop that started with Rahim closes on Anwar's phone. `[A12 · Problem 6 · Closing Loop]` The low-paper row also offers **"Create PO"** pre-filled, in case he'd rather order himself. `[A14 · Problem 6 · Closing Loop]` A **bell** with a count holds alerts open until resolved. `[A18 + S7 · Problem 4 · Closing Loop]` *(S7 notification centre ships at launch — Q6.)* He closes the app in under forty seconds, having actually **done** the thing Rahim needed.

> **Single-approver risk (A12/A13, S8):** Anwar is the only approver. If he's on leave, Rahim's PO waits and the warehouse stalls. Backup / delegate / 48-hour escalation must be decided. Phase 2.

**14.4.3 — The Phase 1 version of this screen.** On day one there's no history and no pending POs — the urgent band would be empty. Instead of ten blank boxes, the top band shows **setup progress**: *"4 of 6 steps done — add Rio Tower's remaining units next."* `[A11 Phase 1 · Problem 6 · Guided Empty State]`

### 14.5 Salam — Viewer. Twice a month. (He checks the tower he invested in.)

**Who he is:** 55, an outside investor in **Rio Tower**. Read-only. Opens the app maybe twice a month, remembers **nothing** in between, wants one number then to leave. No interest in the paper warehouse and should never see it.

**14.5.1 — A screen that explains itself.** Salam logs in with zero memory of last time, so the product carries no *learned behaviour* — nothing hidden in a menu he's meant to recall. He lands straight on **Rio Tower** — his slice, not Anwar's whole company. Each unit shows its **status in plain words** — available / reserved / sold — and the headline he came for, **"18 of 24 sold,"** is the biggest thing on screen. `[V1 + V3 · Problem 1 · Speaking Number]`

> Real estate speaks **status**, not a quantity. Speaking Number here means *the one figure that answers his question is the loudest thing on the page.*

**14.5.2 — Never a locked door.** A **read-only** world with an obvious **Export** where allowed; everything he *can't* do is **hidden, not greyed out** — he never taps a button only to be told no, and never sees cost prices, suppliers, margins, or a carton of paper. `[V2 · Problem 4 · Guided Empty State]`

> **Open scope flag (V1 / Q21):** is "Viewer" strictly internal, or genuinely client-facing? If client-facing (a buyer browsing), this becomes a separate, more guarded view — new scope. Story assumes a trusted investor for now.

### 14.6 What ties all four stories together

Read in order, the four people share **one company and one chain of work**:

1. **Anwar builds** the world — one firm, two item types, via the multi-select gate.
2. **Rahim works** the warehouse half and, when paper runs low, **raises a PO.**
3. **Anwar approves that same PO** the next morning, from his phone, in seconds.
4. **Salam views** only the Rio Tower half — the other thing Anwar built on day one.

They prove the brief — *"the system knows more than it says; our job is to make it speak"* — from four sides: Anwar setup needs it to speak a **path** and hold **two business types honestly**; Rahim needs it to speak the **available number** and speak **honestly** about mess; Anwar daily needs it to speak **priority** and let him act on the spot; Salam needs it to speak **plainly**, showing only his slice. Every one of the four concepts appears in at least two people's stories.
