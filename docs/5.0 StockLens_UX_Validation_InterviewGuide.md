# StockLens — Validation Interview Guide

**Module:** VantaTrack StockLens (Inventory)
**Stage:** Validate (the ⬜ row in `4.0 StockLens_UX_Ideate_and_UserFlows.md`)
**Purpose:** 3–5 interviews with real inventory people, so we can confirm or kill the assumptions in doc 4.0 before we draw flow diagrams and wireframes.
**Status:** Ready to run.

---

## 1. What this study is for

Doc 4.0 is full of good-looking guesses. The personas are marked "assumptions." The eight problems were reasoned, not observed. Before we spend weeks on diagrams and wireframes built on those guesses, we test the guesses on real people.

**The one decision this study drives:**

> If real inventory people do NOT feel the problems we designed for — especially the "wrong number" pain and the mobile way of working — we change what we build first, and we drop whatever solves a problem nobody has.

So every question below exists to confirm or kill one thing in the doc. If a question doesn't map to a decision, it's not here.

---

## 2. Who to interview (this matters more than the questions)

**Recruit 3–5 people who track physical stock for a living:**

- 3–4 **staff / storekeepers** — the people who move stock all day (this is "Rahim"). They are the main test.
- 1–2 **owners / managers** — the people who set it up and check it (this is "Anwar").

**Do NOT interview:**

- Real-estate agents. The property flow is Phase 3 in the PRD (needs Pulse). It is not what we build first, so it is not what we validate first.
- Office people who only *look at* reports and never touch stock.

**Why so narrow:** five interviews spread across five industries teaches nothing about any one of them. One bleeding wound, looked at closely, beats five paper cuts.

---

## 3. The two rules that decide whether this works

**Rule 1 — Ask about the past, never the future.**
People cannot predict their own behavior. "Would you use a phone app?" gets a polite yes and a lie. "Show me how you did the last stock count" gets the truth, because it already happened.

**Rule 2 — Do not sell. Do not lead.**
Never describe our solution and ask if they like it. They will be nice to you. Ask about their problem; let them tell you if it hurts. If you catch yourself saying "don't you think it would be better if…" — stop.

---

## 4. The interview — opening

Keep it human. Two minutes.

> "Thanks for the time. I'm not here to show you anything or sell anything. I just want to understand how you actually handle stock day to day — the real way, mess included. There are no wrong answers; if something is annoying, I want to hear that most of all. Mind if I record so I don't have to scribble?"

Then: "To start — what's your role, and how long have you been doing it?"

---

## 5. The core questions

Five main questions. Each one is a story prompt about the last time something happened. Under each: what it validates in doc 4.0, and a follow-up to dig deeper.

Ask them to *tell the story*. Stay quiet. Let silence do the work.

---

### Q1 — The whole day (validates the persona + the mobile bet)

> "Walk me through yesterday, from when you arrived to when you left. Every single time you looked something up or wrote something down about stock — I want each one."

**Validates:** Are the personas real? How often is stock touched? And the big one — **what device do they reach for?** Do not ask about phone vs computer. Just watch what they name on their own.

**Follow-up:** "That moment you checked the stock — what did you have in your hand? Where were you standing?"

*(Decision this feeds: mobile-first or not — the thing you said you're most afraid is wrong.)*

---

### Q2 — The broken promise (validates Problem 1 — the highest-value bet in the doc)

> "Tell me about a time you told someone you had something — a customer, a colleague, your boss — and then it turned out you didn't really have it, or not enough."

**Validates:** Problem 1 (the "available vs on-hand vs reserved" number). This is the single most expensive problem in the doc. If nobody has this story, the whole "Speaking Number" concept is over-weighted.

**Follow-up:** "What number were you looking at when you made that promise? Where did you see it? What did the number actually mean?"

---

### Q3 — Finding one thing, fast (validates Problem 7 — search)

> "Think of the last time you had to find one specific item quickly while somebody was standing there waiting. What did you actually do, step by step?"

**Validates:** Problem 7 (search) and the "answer in the result row" idea. Listen for: did they search, walk, call someone, or just *know*? A typo panic? Too many lookalikes?

**Follow-up:** "What made it slow? What would have made it instant?"

---

### Q4 — When the delivery is wrong (validates Problem 8 — partial receipt)

> "Tell me about the last delivery that didn't match the order — fewer than expected, extra, wrong item, damaged. What did you do, and what did you do in whatever system you use?"

**Validates:** Problem 8 and Decision 6 (partial receipt as the default, the 18-of-20 case). Confirms whether "how many arrived?" is really the normal case or a rare one.

**Follow-up:** "How often does a delivery come in exactly as ordered, honestly? Half the time? Less?"

---

### Q5 — Today's tool and its day one (validates Problem 2 + the whole "is tracking the pain" decision)

> "How is stock tracked where you work right now — what do you actually use? And do you remember how that got set up in the first place?"

**Validates:** Problem 2 (day one / onboarding) and the core decision: **is inventory tracking genuinely their pain, or is something else worse?** If they say "Excel is fine, our real problem is X" — that's gold, and it may redirect the whole build.

**Follow-up:** "What's the most annoying part of that tool? When did you last want to throw it out the window?"

---

### For the owner/manager only — Q6 (validates Problem 6 — the glance dashboard)

> "First thing in the morning, what do you check about your stock or your business — and where do you look for it?"

**Validates:** Problem 6 (two-zone dashboard, urgent-first) and the "Anwar checks in 40 seconds" persona.

**Follow-up:** "What are you hoping to catch before it becomes a problem? What's bitten you before because you saw it too late?"

---

## 6. Closing

Two questions that always earn their keep.

> "If you had a magic wand and could fix one thing about handling stock — no limits — what would it be?"

> "Is there anything I should have asked about but didn't?"

Then thank them.

---

## 7. What each answer changes (the decision map)

Bring this to the debrief after all interviews. For each, tally what you actually heard.

- **Most reached for a phone / worked standing up** → mobile-first is confirmed. Build Rahim's screens for the hand and the thumb.
- **Most sat at a desk, used a computer, hated the idea of a phone** → the mobile bet is wrong. Redesign staff screens desktop-first; this is a big, early save.
- **The broken-promise story (Q2) was common and painful** → keep "Speaking Number" as the #1 concept.
- **Nobody had a broken-promise story** → demote it; it's a solution to a rare problem.
- **Finding things was slow and stressful (Q3)** → search stays a top-priority screen.
- **Wrong deliveries were common (Q4)** → partial receipt as default is confirmed.
- **They said tracking is fine, the real pain is elsewhere (Q5)** → stop and rethink scope before wireframes. This is the most important thing you can learn.

---

## 8. What these interviews CANNOT decide (do not waste interview time here)

These are in doc 4.0 as open questions, but they are **product-owner / business calls**, not things a storekeeper can answer. Take them to the owner meeting, not the interviews:

- Phase 1 stock-in yes/no (doc Q1)
- Items page default location (doc Q2)
- One list vs tabs per item type (doc Q4)
- Staff-can-create-PO default, and sub-threshold auto-send (doc Q11, Q12)
- Single-approver backup / escalation (doc 14.7 item 7)

Interviews tell you what people *do and feel*. They do not tell you what the business *should decide*. Keep the two meetings separate.

---

## 9. Practical notes

- 30–45 minutes each. Longer and both of you fade.
- One interviewer talks, ideally a second person takes notes.
- Record if allowed. Write up within the same day, while memory is fresh.
- After each interview, write one line: "The biggest surprise was ___." Those surprises are the whole point.
